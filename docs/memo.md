# Memo to Kwame Boateng: favour franchise bundles, with a modest lead

**Date:** 20 November 2026 · **Data as of:** TMDb, 20 November 2026 · **From:** Jonathan, Data Engineering

## Recommendation
**Prioritise franchise bundles, but keep room for strong standalone films.** On your list, a typical
franchise film earned more at the box office for every dollar it cost to make than a typical
standalone film, both for films since 2000 and across all years. None of our data-cleaning choices
reverses this. The lead is modest, and only 12 recent standalone films are on the list, so treat it
as a clear lean, not a certainty.

## The comparison, both ways
"Return" = box office ÷ budget (5x = earned five times its cost). The "typical film" is the middle
film when ranked by return, so one huge hit can't distort it.

| | Since 2000 | All years |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Typical franchise film (films counted) | **5.1x** (59) | 5.5x (87) |
| Typical standalone film (films counted) | **4.5x** (12) | 5.0x (25) |
| All franchise money added up | 5.1x | 5.5x |
| All standalone money added up | 4.4x | **5.6x** |

Since 2000, franchises lead on every measure, and a typical franchise film also earns far more in
total ($796M vs $450M). Across all years, adding up all the money puts standalones slightly ahead,
because of a few old, exceptional hits such as Jaws (67x). We lead with films since 2000 because
older box office comes from a very different market, as Finance has pointed out.

**Franchise bundle** here means 2 or more films of the same series on your list, which is what you
could buy as one deal. A single film from a series (e.g. Jaws) counts as a standalone. Counting it as a
franchise would flip the all-years "money added up" result to franchises.

## Shortlist to negotiate from
**Franchise bundles**
- **The Lord of the Rings (3 films, 2001–03):** the strongest bundle. Typical film 11.7x; even the weakest earned 9.4x.
- **Ice Age (3 films, 2002–09):** typical film 8.3x, weakest 6.5x. Reliable, recent family titles.
- **The Avengers (4 films, 2012–19):** the most recent bundle. Typical film 6.9x, weakest 6.0x, and $7.9B in total box office, a sign of strong demand.

**Standalone films (all since 2000)**
- **Spirited Away (2001):** 14.5x, the best recent standalone on the list.
- **The Incredibles (2004):** 6.9x, a family film with wide appeal.
- **Inception (2010):** 5.2x, the second-largest audience among standalones (by number of TMDb ratings).
- **Monsters, Inc. (2001):** 5.0x, a well-rated family film.
- **Interstellar (2014):** 4.5x, the largest audience among standalones.

## Talent that signals a title worth licensing
Most top names point back to one successful series: Star Wars and Lord of the Rings actors rank high
because those films did well. The stronger signal is people with hits **across different series**:
**Harrison Ford** (8 films across Star Wars and Indiana Jones, typical film 14.8x) and director
**Steven Spielberg** (8 films, 13.3x). The most recent names are directors **Anthony and Joe Russo**
(2 films, 2019, 7.6x). Use talent to choose between similar titles, not as a reason on its own:
most names rest on only 2 or 3 films.

## Deal note: The Lord of the Rings
The bundle worth paying the most for, relative to its size. Its typical film earned 11.7x, about
twice the typical film on your list (5.4x), and its weakest film (9.4x) beat the typical film of
almost every other bundle. All three films are from 2001–03, so Finance's concern about old box
office doesn't apply. **The number that drives this view: a 9.4x floor, with no weak film to carry.**
The caution: it's only three films, over 20 years old, so many viewers have seen them. Its value is
in re-watching, not discovery.
