# Memo to Kwame Boateng: put the $14M behind franchise bundles first

**Date:** 20 November 2026 · **Data as of:** TMDb film database, 20 November 2026 · **From:** Jonathan, Data Engineering

## Recommendation
**Prioritise franchise bundles, and keep a smaller share for the strongest standalone films.** On your
list, a typical franchise film earned more per dollar of budget than a typical standalone film.
**This holds since 2000 and across all years, on every measure**, and no data-cleaning choice reverses
it. The caution: only 8 of your standalone films are from 2000 onward, so that side rests on little evidence.

## The comparison, both ways
"Return" = box office ÷ budget (5x = earned five times its cost). The "typical film" is the middle film
when ranked by return, so one huge hit can't distort it.

| | Since 2000 | All years |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Typical franchise film (films counted) | **5.1x** (63) | 5.6x (96) |
| Typical standalone film (films counted) | **3.8x** (8) | 3.8x (16) |
| All franchise money added up | 5.1x | 5.6x |
| All standalone money added up | 4.2x | 4.9x |

Both windows give the same answer. We lead with 2000 onward because older box office comes from a
different market. A film counts as franchise if it belongs to a series, since studios sell a series
only as a whole bundle.

## Shortlist to negotiate from
**Franchise bundles** (2+ films on your list, so the bundle can be judged)
- **The Lord of the Rings (3 films, 2001–03):** the strongest bundle. Typical film 11.7x; even the weakest earned 9.4x.
- **Ice Age (3 films, 2002–09):** typical film 8.3x, weakest 6.5x. Consistent, recent family titles.
- **The Avengers (4 films, 2012–19):** the most recent bundle. Weakest film 6.0x, and $7.9 billion in total box office.

**Standalone films (all since 2000)**
- **Spirited Away (2001):** 14.5x, by far the best recent standalone.
- **Inception (2010):** 5.2x, the second-largest audience of any standalone.
- **Interstellar (2014):** 4.5x, the largest audience of any standalone.
- **Memento (2000):** 4.5x from a $9M budget, a low-cost title with a strong reputation.
- **The Departed (2006):** 3.2x, a well-reviewed drama that earned $291M.

## Talent that signals a title worth licensing
Most top names just point back to one hit series. The stronger signal is people with hits **across
different series**: **Harrison Ford** (8 films
across Star Wars and Indiana Jones, typical film 14.8x) and director **Steven Spielberg** (8 films,
13.3x). The most recent names are directors **Anthony and Joe Russo** (2 films, 2019, 7.6x). Use
talent to choose between similar titles: most names rest on only 2 or 3 films.

## Deal note: The Lord of the Rings
Pay more per film for this bundle than for any other on the list. Its typical film earned 11.7 times
its budget, about twice the typical title on your list (5.4x), and its weakest film (9.4x) beat the
typical film of almost every other bundle. All three films are from 2001–03, so Finance's concern
about old figures doesn't apply. **The number that drove this view: a 9.4x floor, with no weak entry
in the bundle to carry.** The caution: the films are over 20 years old, so many viewers have seen them.
